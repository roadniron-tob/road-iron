        Workout plans

        Recover & train smarter

        Train anywhere

        No gym? Use what's around you. Pick the Backpack, Travel Bands, Parking Lot or No Equipment plan in Workout plans.

        Weights you already have

        • Backpack: fill it with water bottles (1 liter ≈ 2.2 lb), books or your laptop. Hug it for squats, hold it by the top strap for rows and Romanian deadlifts, wear it for push-ups, step-ups and rucking.
        • Gallon water jugs: about 8.3 lb each, with handles, so they work like kettlebells for carries, curls, rows and goblet squats.
        • Grocery bags or a duffel: load and carry. Farmer's carries build grip, core and shoulders.
        • Rolling suitcase: drag it for a carry, or lift it by the handle for deadlifts.
        • Resistance bands (about $20): the best travel buy. They give real resistance for presses, rows, pulldowns, curls and glute work.

        Around a parking lot, rest stop or park

        • Curb: step-ups and calf raises with your heels hanging off.
        • Park bench or picnic table: incline push-ups, bench dips, step-ups and box squats. Check that it's solid first.
        • Truck tailgate or a sturdy rail: incline push-ups. Never use the car hood; it dents and slides.
        • Stairs or a parking deck ramp: the best low-impact cardio there is. Walk up, rest at the top, walk down.
        • Hotel room: bed for hip thrusts and box squats, desk for incline push-ups, wall for wall sits, and a chair for step-ups only if it can't roll.

        Keep it safe at your weight

        • Skip jumping and running for now. Step jacks, stairs and rucking give the same heart-rate boost with much less knee stress.
        • On long drives, stop every 2 hours for 5 minutes of squats, calf raises and a walk. It helps blood sugar and circulation.
        Recovery
        • Sleep 7–9 hours. Muscle is repaired while you sleep, and short nights raise hunger and blood sugar the next day.
        • 48–72 hours before training the same muscle hard again. Full body and upper/lower plans have this built in.
        • Protein spread over the day: 3–4 meals with 40–50 g each. Greek yogurt or cottage cheese before bed works well.
        • Walk on rest days. Easy movement clears soreness faster than lying still.
        • Deload every 6–8 weeks: one week with half the sets and lighter weights. You come back stronger.
        • Drink enough water that your urine is pale yellow, and more on training days and hot days.
        • Soreness in the first weeks is normal. Sharp joint pain, or pain that gets worse during a set, is not: stop and switch the exercise.
        • Alcohol and poor sleep both slow muscle repair.
        Vitamins & minerals

        With your labs, ask your doctor before starting supplements. Food first, then fill gaps.

        • Vitamin D: often low, and it matters for muscle, mood and blood sugar. Get it tested. Sources are sun, salmon, eggs, or a supplement your doctor doses.
        • Iron: you're low. Eat red meat, liver pâté, beans and spinach with peppers or tomatoes, and keep tea and coffee away from those meals. Take iron pills only if your doctor says to. Women need 18 mg a day (ages 19–50); men need 8 mg.
        • Magnesium: helps muscle function and sleep. Nuts, seeds, beans and leafy greens. Magnesium glycinate is a gentle supplement.
        • Omega-3: salmon twice a week helps triglycerides. High-dose fish oil for triglycerides should be doctor-guided.
        • Potassium: vegetables, potatoes, beans and yogurt. Helps with cramps and blood pressure.
        • Calcium & vitamin B12: dairy, eggs, meat and fish. Calcium matters especially for women's bones.
        • Creatine monohydrate, 3–5 g a day: the best-researched supplement for strength and muscle. Expect 2–4 lb of water weight at first. Ask your doctor if you have kidney problems.
        Peak intensity
        • Effort: end your working sets with 1–3 reps left in the tank. That's what makes muscle grow; going to total failure adds little and slows recovery.
        • Reps: 6–12 builds muscle well, and 12–20 is just as good and easier on joints. Strength work uses 3–6.
        • Sets: 10–20 hard sets per muscle a week. Beginners grow on 6–10.
        • Rest: 2–3 minutes on big lifts, 60–90 seconds on smaller ones.
        • Progress: add reps first. When every set hits the top of the range, add weight: about 5 lb on upper-body lifts and 10 lb on legs.
        • Tempo: lower the weight over 2–3 seconds. Control beats speed.
        • Breathing: brace, then breathe out as you push. Long breath-holds spike blood pressure.
        • Cardio zones: Zone 2 (fat-burning, easy talk) is about 60–70% of max heart rate. Add your age in Goal & roadmap to see your numbers.
        • Time of day: strength often peaks in the late afternoon, but the best time is the one you'll keep.
        • Warm-up: 5 minutes of walking, then 1–2 lighter sets of your first exercise.
        Male vs female differences

        Same for everyone

        • Muscle grows by the same rules: hard sets, progressive overload, enough protein and sleep.
        • Protein needs per pound of goal weight are about the same.

        Men

        • Higher testosterone means more muscle and strength gain in absolute terms.
        • More fat stored around the belly and organs, which is linked to blood sugar and triglycerides, and is usually the first fat to go.
        • Healthy body fat for men is about 14–24%; athletic is 6–13%.
        • Iron need is 8 mg a day.

        Women

        • Gain about the same muscle relative to body size, but less in absolute terms. Lifting heavy won't make you bulky.
        • Often recover faster between sets and handle more reps at a given weight, so higher volume works well.
        • Many feel strongest in the first half of the cycle (follicular phase). Adjust by feel; there's no need to plan around it.
        • Need more iron (18 mg a day, ages 19–50) and more attention to calcium and vitamin D for bones.
        • Healthy body fat for women is about 21–31%; athletic is 14–20%. Below about 14% risks losing periods and bone density.
        • Wider hips change the knee angle. Glute and hamstring work keeps knees tracking straight.

        Meals you'll actually eat

        Breakfast
        • 3–4 eggs scrambled with peppers, tomato and onion, 1 slice sesame bread. About 30 g protein. Peppers and tomato help you absorb the iron from the eggs.
        • Liver pâté on 1 slice of bread with tomato and pepper slices, 2 boiled eggs on the side. Pâté is one of the best iron foods you can buy. Have your tea an hour later, not with it.
        • Ajvar and beans on toast with 2–3 fried or boiled eggs. Beans bring iron and fiber, which slows the blood sugar rise from the bread.
        • Greek yogurt with berries and a spoon of peanut butter. Fast, cooler-friendly, 20+ g protein.
        On the road
        • Sushi: sashimi or nigiri, a seaweed salad or miso soup. Go easy on tempura, cream cheese and spicy mayo rolls; they can double the calories.
        • Culver's: Garden Fresco salad with grilled chicken, or a grilled chicken sandwich. If it's a ButterBurger day, get a single, skip the fries and curds, and have a side salad.
        • Walmart run: rotisserie chicken, bagged salad, cherry tomatoes, baby carrots. Two or three meals for under $15.
        • Tuna spread: tuna pouch, a little light mayo, chopped onion, on 1 slice of bread or lettuce leaves. Half regular mayo, half Greek yogurt keeps the taste and cuts the fat.
        • Shrimp cocktail tray or a pre-cooked shrimp bag with a bell pepper. Almost pure protein.
        Your restaurants
        • Olive Garden: chicken & gnocchi soup with the house salad, dressing on the side, one breadstick. About 520 calories but only about 18 g protein, so have a protein shake or jerky later.
        • Panera: chicken noodle soup with a half Green Goddess Cobb with chicken. About 440 calories and 35 g protein. The whole Cobb on its own works too.
        • McDonald's: two cheeseburgers, no fries, zero-sugar drink. About 600 calories and 30 g protein. The fries and regular soda are what make McDonald's heavy.
        • Panda Express: Beijing Beef plus Grilled Teriyaki Chicken, with Super Greens as the side. Beijing Beef with a full chow mein is about 1,070 calories and 140 g carbs. This swap keeps the beef and gives you much more protein. Can't skip the noodles? Ask for half chow mein, half Super Greens.
        • Steakhouse: 6–8 oz sirloin, steamed broccoli or asparagus, side salad. If you want the potato, get it plain with a little butter instead of loaded.
        • Applebee's: Blackened Cajun salmon. About 600 calories with 43 g protein, and the salmon is good for your triglycerides. Bourbon Street chicken is close to 800; swap its potatoes for broccoli.
        • Wawa: chicken hoagie Shorti, or 3 chicken strips with a side salad and the noodle soup. The mashed potato bowl alone is about 520 calories and 32 g fat. Share it or get a small.
        Home cooking
        • Steak with asparagus and a small scoop of your mashed potatoes. Your $14–18 Walmart steak gives 2–3 meals. Red meat is the easiest iron there is.
        • Pork schnitzel, air-fried instead of pan-fried, with a tomato, cucumber and pepper salad. Same crunch, about half the fat. Pound the $5–6 slices thin and bread them with egg and panko.
        • Frozen salmon, pan-seared or air-fried, with carrots and a small potato. Salmon twice a week helps bring triglycerides down.
        • Shrimp and pepper stir-fry with onion and frozen veggies over a small cup of rice.
        • Lighter Olivier salad: more chicken and eggs, fewer potatoes, half mayo and half Greek yogurt, with peas, carrots, corn and pickles. Keeps the flavor you like and doubles the protein.
        • Beef and bean chili with peppers and tomatoes. Iron from the beef and beans, freezes well, easy to take on the road.
        • Sheet-pan chicken thighs with peppers, onion and carrots.
        Keeping your sweets

        You can keep all of these. The trick is size and how often, and planning 2–3 treats a week instead of every day.

        Pepsi→Pepsi Zero Sugar Nutella from the jar→1 level tbsp on toast with eggs Magnum, any night→Magnum mini, or a full bar on a planned night Cheetos, big bag→1 oz bag, or baked chips

        Regular Pepsi is the one that matters most. A 12 oz can is about 40 g of sugar, which hits both blood sugar and triglycerides. If you switch only one thing, switch that.

        Where you stand

        PrediabetesTrack A1C and fasting glucose. A1C 5.7–6.4% is the prediabetes range. Losing 5–7% of your body weight lowers the risk of it turning into diabetes a lot.
        High triglyceridesSugary drinks, sweets, white bread and alcohol push these up. Salmon, walking and weight loss bring them down.
        Low ironTrack ferritin, iron and hemoglobin. In men, low iron is worth having your doctor look into the cause, and take iron pills only if they tell you to.
        TestosteroneExtra body fat lowers testosterone; losing weight, lifting, sleeping 7+ hours and treating sleep apnea all raise it. Test it in the morning (7–10 am), and repeat a low result before acting. Skip "T-booster" supplements and talk to your doctor about low results.
        Blood pressureCheck it at a pharmacy machine or with a home cuff and add it below. Aim under 130/80. Holding your breath on heavy lifts spikes it, so breathe out as you push.

        Ask for at your next test

        • A1C, fasting glucose and fasting insulin: track prediabetes.
        • Lipid panel: triglycerides, HDL, LDL.
        • Iron panel with ferritin, and a CBC: your low iron and hemoglobin.
        • Comprehensive metabolic panel: liver (ALT) and kidneys (eGFR). Useful with high protein and if you try creatine.
        • Total and free testosterone, drawn in the morning.
        • TSH: thyroid, which affects weight and energy.
        • Vitamin D and B12.
        • hs-CRP and uric acid: inflammation and gout risk.
        • And a sleep study if your watch shows regular snoring.

        Retest A1C and lipids every 3 months while you're losing weight. Your doctor decides what's needed for you.

        On your plate

        For blood sugar and triglycerides

        • Swap regular soda for zero-sugar.
        • Eat protein and vegetables first, bread or potatoes last and smaller.
        • A 10–15 minute walk after meals lowers the blood sugar spike.
        • Salmon twice a week. Go light on alcohol.

        For iron

        • Steak, beef, liver pâté, eggs, beans and shrimp.
        • Pair them with peppers or tomatoes; vitamin C helps you absorb iron.
        • Tea and coffee block iron. Drink them an hour before or after iron-rich meals.
